Oklahoma 30-Day Notice to End a Month-to-Month Tenancy (Landlord Guide)

Oklahoma statutes on OSCN · last checked 2026-10-19 · informational, not legal advice

Quick answer

Short answer: Under 41 O.S. §111, a month-to-month (or at-will) tenancy in Oklahoma ends with 30 days' written notice, counted from the date of service. Tenancies shorter than month-to-month (for example week-to-week) take 7 days' written notice. A fixed-term lease ends on its end date without notice unless the lease says otherwise.

Notice must be served the way §111(E) lists: personal delivery, then a household family member over 12, then posting and mailing.

What the law says

  • Month-to-month or at will: either party may end it with 30 days' written notice. The 30 days run from the date of service. 41 O.S. §111 · last checked 2026-10-19
  • Shorter than month-to-month (for example week-to-week): 7 days' written notice. 41 O.S. §111 · last checked 2026-10-19
  • Fixed-term lease: ends on its end date without notice, unless the lease says otherwise. 41 O.S. §111 · last checked 2026-10-19
  • Holdover: if the tenant stays after the tenancy ends, the landlord may sue for possession right away. If the holdover is willful and not in good faith, the landlord can also recover up to 2× the average monthly rent, prorated daily, for the holdover period. If the landlord consents to the tenant staying, the tenancy becomes month-to-month. 41 O.S. §111 · last checked 2026-10-19
  • Domestic violence, sexual violence or stalking (§111(F), added 2021): a victim may end the lease without penalty by giving written notice plus a protective order within 30 days of the incident, unless the landlord waives the time period. 41 O.S. §111 · last checked 2026-10-19
  • Occupants with no agreement and no consent (§111(G)): the landlord may demand they leave without an eviction case; refusing within a reasonable time is trespass. Applying this to people who were once guests is risky; ask counsel. 41 O.S. §111 · last checked 2026-10-19

How to serve the notice (§111(E))

The statute lists the methods in order: 41 O.S. §111 · last checked 2026-10-19

  1. Deliver it to the tenant personally.
  2. If the tenant can't be found, deliver it to a family member over 12 who lives there.
  3. Otherwise, post it in a conspicuous place AND mail a copy by certified mail or USPS Firm Mailing Book for Accountable Mail.

How experienced landlords do it

Practice (not law): Hand notices only to an adult 18 or older. If no adult is present, post AND mail. This is stricter than the statute, which allows a household family member over 12 (41 O.S. §111(E)).

Practice notes come from John Ogorzalek, 5 Dogs Properties (owned 300+ units since 2014). They are habits, not substitutes for the statute.

Checklist

  1. Confirm the tenancy type: month-to-month or at will (30 days), shorter than month-to-month (7 days), or fixed term (check the lease end date and any notice clause).
  2. Write the notice. Count the days from the date you will serve it.
  3. Serve it in the §111(E) order: personal delivery, household family member over 12, or post AND certified/accountable mail.
  4. If the tenant stays past the end date, possession goes through the court process, not self-help. See the eviction process and eviction timeline.
  5. Before acting on a domestic-violence termination, a federally backed or subsidized unit, or anything unusual, talk to a licensed Oklahoma attorney.

FAQ

How much notice does an Oklahoma landlord have to give to end a month-to-month tenancy?

30 days' written notice, counted from the date of service (41 O.S. §111).

Do I have to give notice when a fixed-term lease ends in Oklahoma?

Under 41 O.S. §111, a fixed-term tenancy ends on its end date without notice unless the lease says otherwise. Read your lease's notice and renewal clauses.

Can I text or email a 30-day notice in Oklahoma?

41 O.S. §111 subsection (E) lists personal delivery, delivery to a household family member over 12, or posting plus certified mail or USPS Firm Mailing Book. Text and email are not on that list; ask a licensed Oklahoma attorney before relying on them.

Can I give a 30-day notice for any reason?

The statute pack used for this page does not cover retaliation or discrimination limits on ending a tenancy. Fair Housing Act rules and lease terms can apply; ask a licensed Oklahoma attorney.
