Oklahoma Tenant Left Belongings Behind? Abandoned Property Rules for Landlords (41 O.S. §130)

41 O.S. §130 last read on OSCN 2026-11-10 (CiteID 71779; last amended Laws 2019, HB 2395) · informational, not legal advice

Informational, not legal advice. This page summarizes what the statute says in plain English. It doesn't tell you how a court would rule on your facts. Talk to a licensed Oklahoma attorney before you act.

Quick answer

Short answer: After a tenant abandons or surrenders the unit, or after a lawful eviction, 41 O.S. §130 lets the landlord dispose of property with no apparent value. For property with apparent value, send the tenant written notice by certified mail to the last-known address with a deadline to remove it and a warning that if the property is not removed within the time specified, it will be deemed abandoned. Property left 30 days or more is conclusively abandoned.

Store it with reasonable care in the meantime. A landlord who follows §130 isn't liable for the disposal; one who deliberately or negligently violates it is liable for actual damages. Before the tenancy has ended, removing belongings is a self-help lockout, not §130.

What the law says (§130)

  • When it applies: after abandonment, surrender, or lawful eviction. 41 O.S. §130 · last checked 2026-11-10
  • (A) No apparent value: the landlord may dispose of it. Perishables may be disposed of as the landlord sees fit. 41 O.S. §130 · last checked 2026-11-10
  • (B) Apparent value: send the tenant written notice by certified mail to the last-known address, giving a deadline to remove it. The notice must warn that if the property is not removed within the time specified, it will be deemed abandoned. Property left 30 days or more is conclusively abandoned. 41 O.S. §130 · last checked 2026-11-10
  • No required sale: §130 does not require a sale or an accounting of proceeds. After 30 days or more the landlord may dispose of the property in any manner deemed reasonable and proper without liability (§130(B)). Subsection (E) lists destroy, sell, or otherwise dispose as a complying landlord's election. 41 O.S. §130 · last checked 2026-11-10
  • (C) Storage: store it with reasonable care. Storage in the unit can't cost more than fair rental value; commercial storage costs the actual charge for the storage and removal. 41 O.S. §130 · last checked 2026-11-10
  • (D) Reclaimed in time: the landlord may charge storage costs plus other costs accrued under the lease. 41 O.S. §130 · last checked 2026-11-10
  • (E) Liability: a landlord who complies isn't liable for disposal. A landlord who deliberately or negligently violates §130 is liable for actual damages. 41 O.S. §130 · last checked 2026-11-10

Related rules: lockouts, re-renting, deposits

  • No self-help during a tenancy: removing a tenant's belongings or locking them out is wrongful removal or exclusion under 41 O.S. §123: the tenant may recover possession or end the lease, and recover up to the greater of 2× average monthly rent or 2× actual damages. 41 O.S. §123 · last checked 2026-10-26
  • Abandoned mid-lease: if the tenant abandons the unit mid-term, the landlord must make reasonable efforts to re-rent. 41 O.S. §129 · last checked 2026-10-14
  • After an eviction judgment: the writ gives the tenant 48 hours' notice before possession is restored, and the sheriff or local police can be summoned to assist. 12 O.S. §1148.10A · last checked 2026-10-14
  • Security deposit: the deposit can go to accrued rent and damages, itemized in a written statement; the balance is due within 45 days after the tenancy ends, possession is delivered, and the tenant makes a written demand. See Oklahoma security deposit rules. 41 O.S. §115 · last checked 2026-10-19

What this page doesn't cover

  • How to tell whether a unit has been "abandoned" (versus a tenant who is just away). The statute summary used here doesn't define it. If in doubt, ask counsel or use the court process.
  • Vehicles, manufactured homes, firearms, documents, a deceased tenant's belongings, and storage-cost disputes.
  • County sheriff practice on move-out day after a writ.

Practice (not law)

Practice (not law): After move-out, send a written list of rent-ready expenses (cleaning, repairs beyond wear, rekey) tied to the move-in/out checklist, plus the §115 itemization. The statute baseline is the §115(B) itemization by return-receipt mail or in person.

Practice notes come from John Ogorzalek, 5 Dogs Properties (owned 300+ units since 2014). They are habits, not substitutes for the statute.

Checklist when a tenant leaves things behind

  1. Confirm the tenancy has ended: abandonment, surrender, or a lawful eviction. If not, don't touch the belongings (§123).
  2. Take dated photos of everything left and write an inventory.
  3. Sort items: no apparent value (and perishables) vs. apparent value.
  4. For items with apparent value, send written notice by certified mail to the last-known address with a removal deadline and a warning that if the property is not removed within the time specified, it will be deemed abandoned. Keep the receipt and a copy.
  5. Store them with reasonable care. Track storage costs (in-unit storage capped at fair rental value; commercial storage at the actual charge for storage and removal).
  6. If the tenant reclaims in time, you may charge storage plus other costs accrued under the lease (§130(D)).
  7. Keep the §130 timeline separate from the §115 deposit itemization and 45-day return.

FAQ

What can a landlord do with a tenant's belongings in Oklahoma?

After abandonment, surrender, or lawful eviction, 41 O.S. §130 lets the landlord dispose of items with no apparent value. For items with apparent value, send written notice by certified mail to the last-known address with a deadline to remove them and a warning that if the property is not removed within the time specified, it will be deemed abandoned; property left 30 days or more is conclusively abandoned.

How long does a landlord have to keep a tenant's belongings in Oklahoma?

For property with apparent value, 41 O.S. §130 treats property left 30 days or more as conclusively abandoned, after certified-mail notice with a removal deadline and a warning that property not removed in time will be deemed abandoned.

Can I charge the tenant for storing their things?

Yes, if they reclaim in time: storage costs plus other costs accrued under the lease (41 O.S. §130(D)). In-unit storage can't cost more than fair rental value; commercial storage costs the actual charge for the storage and removal.

Can I throw out a tenant's stuff while they still live there?

No. During a tenancy, removing belongings or locking the tenant out is wrongful removal or exclusion under 41 O.S. §123. 41 O.S. §130 applies only after abandonment, surrender, or lawful eviction.
