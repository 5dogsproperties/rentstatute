Oklahoma Emotional Support Animal and Assistance Animal Rules for Landlords (41 O.S. §113.2)

41 O.S. §113.2 last read 2026-11-10 (Justia 2025) · informational, not legal advice

Informational, not legal advice. This page summarizes what the statute says in plain English. It doesn't tell you how a court would rule on your facts. Talk to a licensed Oklahoma attorney before you act.

Quick answer

Short answer: Oklahoma's 41 O.S. §113.2 treats service animals and emotional support animals for a person with a disability as "assistance animals." Tenants may request a reasonable accommodation under the federal Fair Housing Act and similar laws. Unless the disability or need is readily apparent, the landlord may ask for reliable documentation, and documentation that was bought is presumed fraudulent.

Federal fair housing rules still control how you handle the request. Don't issue a blanket denial, and talk to counsel before you say no. This page covers only the Oklahoma statute.

What the law says (§113.2)

  • Who it covers: "assistance animal" includes service animals and emotional support animals for a person with a disability. 41 O.S. §113.2 · last checked 2026-11-10
  • The request: tenants may request a reasonable accommodation under the federal Fair Housing Act, the ADA, Section 504, and similar laws. 41 O.S. §113.2 · last checked 2026-11-10
  • Documentation: unless the disability or need is readily apparent, the landlord may ask for reliable documentation that (1) verifies the disability under the FHA, (2) describes the accommodation, and (3) shows how the disability relates to the need. 41 O.S. §113.2 · last checked 2026-11-10
  • Verification: the landlord may independently verify the documentation. Documentation that was bought (paid for as goods or services) is presumed fraudulent. 41 O.S. §113.2 · last checked 2026-11-10
  • Liability: the landlord isn't liable for injuries caused by an assistance animal allowed as an accommodation. 41 O.S. §113.2 · last checked 2026-11-10
  • False claims: a knowingly false assistance-animal claim can be handled under 41 O.S. §132 (lease violation). A landlord who prevails in the eviction may get costs and fees plus damages up to $1,000. 41 O.S. §113.2 · last checked 2026-11-10

Federal law controls the request. The Fair Housing Act itself still applies. HUD's detailed assistance-animal notices (FHEO-2020-01 and FHEO-2013-01) were withdrawn effective September 17, 2025 (91 Fed. Reg. 17291). There is no blanket denial, and counsel is needed. Federal rules aren't summarized here.

Practice (not law)

Practice (not law): Use a signed pet addendum (pet fee, pet deposit, rules) for pets. Oklahoma §113.2 does not address pet fees. Do not treat “no pet fees for assistance animals” as settled current HUD guidance: HUD withdrew FHEO-2020-01 (the January 28, 2020 notice that said a housing provider may not charge a deposit, fee, or surcharge for an assistance animal) effective September 17, 2025, and May 22, 2026 FHEO enforcement guidance prioritizes trained animals and no longer treats untrained emotional support animals as categorically entitled to a pet-policy waiver. The Fair Housing Act itself still applies, and private court actions remain possible. Check current HUD materials and counsel before charging or waiving a fee.

Checklist when a tenant asks for an assistance animal

  1. Treat it as a reasonable-accommodation request, not a pet request. Don't issue a blanket denial or apply your pet policy by default.
  2. Is the disability or need readily apparent? If not, §113.2 lets you ask for reliable documentation covering the three points above.
  3. Keep the request, your reply, and any documentation in the tenant file. You may independently verify it; bought documentation is presumed fraudulent (§113.2).
  4. Before denying, or before treating a claim as knowingly false under §132, talk to counsel. The Fair Housing Act itself still applies; check current HUD materials and counsel.
  5. For a regular unauthorized pet, see the lease violation notice page.

FAQ

Are emotional support animals covered by Oklahoma landlord law?

Yes. 41 O.S. §113.2 defines assistance animals to include service animals and emotional support animals for a person with a disability. Tenants may request a reasonable accommodation under the federal Fair Housing Act and similar laws.

Can an Oklahoma landlord ask for an ESA letter?

Unless the disability or need is readily apparent, 41 O.S. §113.2 lets the landlord ask for reliable documentation that verifies the disability under the FHA, describes the accommodation, and shows how the disability relates to the need. The landlord may independently verify it.

Are online ESA letters valid in Oklahoma?

Under 41 O.S. §113.2, documentation that was bought (paid for as goods or services) is presumed fraudulent. How that applies to a specific letter is a question for counsel. The Fair Housing Act itself still applies.

What happens if a tenant lies about an assistance animal in Oklahoma?

A knowingly false claim can be handled under 41 O.S. §132. A landlord who prevails in the eviction may recover costs and fees plus damages up to $1,000 (41 O.S. §113.2). Talk to counsel first.

Is an Oklahoma landlord liable if an assistance animal hurts someone?

41 O.S. §113.2 says the landlord isn't liable for injuries by an assistance animal allowed as an accommodation.
