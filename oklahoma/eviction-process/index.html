How the Oklahoma Eviction Process Works for Landlords

Residential Landlord and Tenant Act (41 O.S.) and forcible entry and detainer provisions (12 O.S. §1148.1 et seq.) on OSCN · last checked 2026-10-27 · informational, not legal advice

Quick answer

Short answer: In Oklahoma, a landlord gives the required written notice (5 days for unpaid rent, 10 days to fix other remediable breaches, or 30 days to end a month-to-month tenancy), files a forcible entry and detainer case with the court clerk, attends a hearing set 5 to 10 days after the summons issues, and, after judgment, has the sheriff restore possession.

Day counts vary by county and case. Each step below cites the statute. Prefer a shorter practical list? See the OK Landlord Hub pre-filing checklist.

Why now: Hoodline reported 76 households with eviction filings in 2025 at one Oklahoma City mobile home park (reported by Hoodline, Oct 23, 2026). MHA Oklahoma reported Oklahoma County hearings set within 5 to 10 days of filing (reported by MHA Oklahoma, Oct 13, 2026). That range matches the statutory trial window in 12 O.S. §1148.4.

Step 1: The notice before filing

  • Unpaid rent: the landlord may terminate the rental agreement if rent is not paid within 5 days after written notice of the landlord's demand. The notice may be given before or after filing, and the demand for rent counts as a demand for possession, so no further notice to quit is needed. 41 O.S. §131 · last checked 2026-10-27 Details: Oklahoma 5-day notice to pay or quit.
  • Other remediable breaches: the tenant has 10 days after written notice (served under §111(E)) to remedy the breach. 41 O.S. §132 · last checked 2026-10-27 Termination timing after an unremedied breach is not covered here; read §132 and ask counsel.
  • Month-to-month tenancy: either side may end it with 30 days' written notice, counted from service under §111(E). Shorter periods for tenancies shorter than month-to-month are in §111(B). 41 O.S. §111 · last checked 2026-10-27
  • Notice content and delivery: beyond the points above, check the current statute text and ask counsel about what your notice must say.

Step 2: Filing the forcible entry and detainer case

  • The case starts with an affidavit filed with the court clerk. Claims for rent and damages can be joined with the claim for possession. 12 O.S. §1148.15 · last checked 2026-10-27
  • If the amount claimed is within the small-claims limit, the case goes on the small-claims docket, where the clerk prepares the affidavit and summons and assists unrepresented plaintiffs. 12 O.S. §1148.14 · last checked 2026-10-27
  • Filing fees: fees vary by county; check your court clerk's fee schedule.

Step 3: Serving the tenant

  • If personal service fails, the statute allows posting plus certified mail at least 5 days before trial. Service this way supports a judgment for possession only. 12 O.S. §1148.5A · last checked 2026-10-27
  • Other service rules and timing: read the current statute text on OSCN or ask counsel. This page does not summarize them.

Step 4: The hearing

  • The summons sets the trial not less than 5 nor more than 10 days from the date the summons is issued. 12 O.S. §1148.4 · last checked 2026-10-27
  • What happens if either side does not appear: ask a licensed Oklahoma attorney. This page does not state an outcome.

Step 5: Judgment, writ, and removal

  • At the plaintiff's request, the court issues a writ to the sheriff. A motion for new trial may be filed only within 3 days of judgment and does not stay execution. 12 O.S. §1148.10 · last checked 2026-10-27
  • The officer gives 48 hours' notice, in person or posted, before restoring possession. Refusing to surrender possession after that is trespass, with a fine up to $500. 12 O.S. §1148.10A · last checked 2026-10-27
  • Appeals and appeal bonds: ask a licensed Oklahoma attorney. This page does not summarize them.

What landlords can't do

The lawful route to possession is the court case and the sheriff's writ. 41 O.S. §123 gives a tenant remedies when a landlord wrongfully removes or excludes them: the tenant may recover possession or terminate the rental agreement, and recover up to the greater of 2× average monthly rent or 2× actual damages. 41 O.S. §123 · last checked 2026-10-27

Partial payment and payment plans

Whether accepting a partial or late payment after notice, or after filing, affects the notice or the case is not answered by the statutes verified for this page. Ask a licensed Oklahoma attorney before accepting money after notice. A written payment plan or move-out agreement is a practical option many owners consider; have counsel review it.

2026: did anything change?

No 2026 bill changed the eviction timeline. SB1209, which would have excluded Sundays and holidays from the eviction (FED) trial period, failed in the House, and no other 2026 bill amending the eviction statutes became law; see Did Oklahoma landlord-tenant law change in 2026?

FAQ

How long does an eviction take in Oklahoma?

It varies by county and case. The verified day counts are: the pre-filing notice period (5 days under 41 O.S. §131, 10 days to remedy under 41 O.S. §132, or 30 days under 41 O.S. §111), a trial set not less than 5 nor more than 10 days from the date the summons is issued (12 O.S. §1148.4), and at least 48 hours' notice before possession is restored (12 O.S. §1148.10A). The statutes do not set a single total.

Do I need a lawyer to file an eviction in Oklahoma?

The statute says a case within the small-claims limit goes on the small-claims docket, where the court clerk prepares the affidavit and summons and assists unrepresented plaintiffs (12 O.S. §1148.14). For contested or complex cases, ask a licensed Oklahoma attorney.

Can I evict for reasons other than unpaid rent?

The statutes cover other grounds. For a remediable breach, 41 O.S. §132 gives the tenant 10 days after written notice to remedy it. A month-to-month tenancy can be ended with 30 days' written notice under 41 O.S. §111. Ask counsel about termination timing after an unremedied breach.

Can I lock out a tenant who hasn't paid?

41 O.S. §123 gives the tenant remedies if a landlord wrongfully removes or excludes them: the tenant may recover possession or terminate the lease, and recover up to the greater of 2× average monthly rent or 2× actual damages. Possession is restored through the court process and the sheriff (12 O.S. §1148.10, 12 O.S. §1148.10A).

What if the tenant pays after I give notice or file?

The statutes verified for this page do not answer whether accepting a partial or late payment affects the notice or the case. Ask a licensed Oklahoma attorney before accepting money after notice.

Can the tenant appeal an eviction judgment?

The statute says a motion for new trial may be filed only within 3 days of judgment and does not stay execution (12 O.S. §1148.10). Appeal and bond rules are not covered on this page; ask a licensed Oklahoma attorney.
