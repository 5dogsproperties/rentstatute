Oklahoma Eviction Timeline for Landlords: How Long Each Step Takes

Oklahoma statutes on OSCN · last checked 2026-10-19 · informational, not legal advice

Quick answer

Short answer: Oklahoma statutes set the day counts for each step, not a single total: the notice period (5 days for unpaid rent, 10 days to remedy other breaches, or 30 days to end a month-to-month tenancy), a trial set 5 to 10 days after the summons is issued, and at least 48 hours' notice before possession is restored after judgment.

How long a real case takes depends on the county, the court calendar, service, and whether the tenant contests. For the full step-by-step, see the Oklahoma eviction process.

Serve pre-filing notices as 41 O.S. §111(E) lists: personal delivery, a household family member over 12, or posting AND certified/accountable mail. 41 O.S. §111 · last checked 2026-10-19 41 O.S. §131 doesn't say how to serve the rent demand; applying §111(E) is common practice, so confirm with counsel.

2026 bills: the proposed changes to the eviction trial window did not become law. SB128 was vetoed in 2025, and SB1209 failed in the House on May 7, 2026, so §1148.4 still reads 5 to 10 days from issuance of the summons. See 2026 Oklahoma law changes.

No lockouts: a landlord who wrongfully removes or excludes a tenant faces the remedies in 41 O.S. §123 (up to the greater of 2× average monthly rent or 2× actual damages). Possession is restored through the court process.

How experienced landlords do it

Practice (not law): Wait until rent is 5 days late before serving the §131 demand, and put the due date and grace period in the lease. This adds days before the statutory clock starts; the statute allows the demand as soon as rent is unpaid when due (41 O.S. §131).

Practice (not law): Hand notices only to an adult 18 or older; if none is present, post AND mail. The statute allows a household family member over 12 (41 O.S. §111(E)).

Practice notes come from John Ogorzalek, 5 Dogs Properties (owned 300+ units since 2014). They are habits, not substitutes for the statute.

Checklist

  1. Pick the right notice for the ground (rent, other breach, or ending month-to-month) and calendar its day count.
  2. Serve it under §111(E) and keep a record of how and when.
  3. After the notice period runs, file with the county court clerk; expect a trial 5 to 10 days after the summons issues.
  4. After judgment, the 48-hour writ notice comes before possession is restored. No self-help at any step.
  5. For contested cases, federally backed or subsidized units, or partial payments after notice, talk to a licensed Oklahoma attorney.

FAQ

How long does it take to evict a tenant in Oklahoma?

The statutes set step-by-step day counts rather than a total: 5 days after a written rent demand (41 O.S. §131), 10 days to remedy other breaches (41 O.S. §132), or 30 days to end a month-to-month tenancy (41 O.S. §111); a trial 5 to 10 days after the summons issues (12 O.S. §1148.4); and 48 hours' notice before possession is restored (12 O.S. §1148.10A). Real cases vary by county and court calendar.

Did Oklahoma change the eviction timeline in 2026?

No. SB1209 failed in the House on May 7, 2026, and SB128 was vetoed in 2025, so the 5-to-10-day trial window in 12 O.S. §1148.4 is unchanged.

When can I serve a 5-day notice for late rent?

41 O.S. §131 allows the written demand once rent is unpaid when due. Many landlords wait until a lease grace period has passed (practice, not law).

What if the tenant pays part of the rent after the notice?

The statutes verified for this page do not answer whether accepting a partial or late payment affects the notice or the case. Ask a licensed Oklahoma attorney before accepting money after notice.
