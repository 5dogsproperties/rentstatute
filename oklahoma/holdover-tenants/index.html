Holdover Tenants in Oklahoma: What 41 O.S. §111(C) and (D) Say

Last checked: 2026-11-24. 41 O.S. §§105, 111, 123 and 12 O.S. §§1148.4, 1148.16 re-read that day on OSCN; statute pack weekly check 2026-11-23 found no change · informational, not legal advice

Informational, not legal advice. This page summarizes what the statute says in plain English. It doesn't tell you how a court would rule on your facts. Talk to a licensed Oklahoma attorney before you act.

Quick answer

Short answer: If a tenant "remains in possession without the landlord's consent after the expiration of the term of the rental agreement or its termination under the Oklahoma Residential Landlord and Tenant Act," the landlord "may immediately bring an action for possession and damages" (41 O.S. §111(D)). A tenancy for a definite term "expires on the ending date thereof without notice" unless it was earlier terminated under the Act or the parties otherwise agreed (§111(C)). 41 O.S. §111 · last checked 2026-11-24

The answer to "tenant won't leave after lease ends" is a court action for possession, not a lockout. A landlord who "wrongfully removes or excludes a tenant from possession" faces a tenant remedy of up to twice the average monthly rental or twice actual damages, whichever is greater (41 O.S. §123). 41 O.S. §123 · last checked 2026-11-24

What the law says

  • Definite term ends on its date (§111(C)). "Unless earlier terminated under the provisions of the Oklahoma Residential Landlord and Tenant Act or unless otherwise agreed upon, a tenancy for a definite term expires on the ending date thereof without notice." 41 O.S. §111 · last checked 2026-11-24
  • Holdover without consent (§111(D)). If the tenant remains in possession without the landlord's consent after the expiration of the term of the rental agreement or its termination under the Act, "the landlord may immediately bring an action for possession and damages." 41 O.S. §111 · last checked 2026-11-24
  • Willful holdover (§111(D)). "If the tenant's holdover is willful and not in good faith the landlord may also recover an amount not more than twice the average monthly rental, computed and prorated on a daily basis, for each month or portion thereof that the tenant remains in possession." That is a ceiling ("not more than"), not an automatic amount. 41 O.S. §111 · last checked 2026-11-24
  • Landlord consent (§111(D)). "If the landlord consents to the tenant's continued occupancy, a month-to-month tenancy is thus created, unless the parties otherwise agree." Subsection D doesn't say what counts as consent. If you aren't sure whether something you did or accepted is consent, ask counsel before you act. 41 O.S. §111 · last checked 2026-11-24
  • Where the landlord can raise it (§105(B)). Rights and remedies declared by the Act are enforceable "in any court of appropriate jurisdiction including small claims court and may be prosecuted as part of an action for forcible entry or detainer," and "the prevailing party shall be entitled to reasonable attorneys' fees" in an action for breach of a rental agreement or to enforce a right or obligation under the Act. 41 O.S. §105 · last checked 2026-11-24

How subsection (D) fits with the notice rules (analysis, not statute text)

  • Subsection D covers the end of the term of a rental agreement and "its termination under" the Act. The Act's termination notices are covered on other pages: 30-day notice for month-to-month and at-will tenancies (§111(A)), 5-day demand for unpaid rent (§131) and lease-violation notices (§132). Whether a particular stay after one of those notices fits subsection D depends on the facts. Ask counsel.
  • How a termination notice is served is in How to serve a notice in Oklahoma (§111(E)).
  • Section 111(G) says the section does not apply to an occupant who has no rental agreement with the landlord and with whom the landlord has not consented to creating a tenancy. See occupants with no lease. 41 O.S. §111 · last checked 2026-11-24

The court process

  • The summons in a forcible entry and detainer (eviction) action sets the trial "not less than five (5) days nor more than ten (10) days from the date that the summons is issued" (12 O.S. §1148.4). 12 O.S. §1148.4 · last checked 2026-11-24
  • The summons must be "in a form developed in plain and understandable language by the Oklahoma Bar Association," and "delivered no less than three (3) days before the scheduled time and date of the court hearing." The hearing "shall not be less than five (5) days from the date the summons is issued" (12 O.S. §1148.16). 12 O.S. §1148.16 · last checked 2026-11-24

Why not change the locks

  • "If a landlord wrongfully removes or excludes a tenant from possession of a dwelling unit, the tenant may recover possession by a proceeding brought in a court of competent jurisdiction, or terminate the rental agreement after giving notice of such intention to the landlord, and in either case recover an amount not more than twice the average monthly rental, or twice his actual damages, whichever is greater." 41 O.S. §123 · last checked 2026-11-24
  • Section 123 doesn't say what counts as "wrongfully." Use the court process in the section above rather than removing the tenant or the tenant's belongings yourself.

How experienced landlords do it

Practice notes come from John Ogorzalek, 5 Dogs Properties (owned 300+ units since 2014). They are habits, not substitutes for the statute.

Practice (not law): When you serve a notice, hand it only to an adult 18 or older. If no adult is there, post it AND mail it. (§111(E) itself allows a household family member over 12; see serving notices.)

Practice (not law): When a tenant moves out, send a written list of rent-ready expenses (cleaning, repairs beyond wear, rekey) tied to the move-in/move-out checklist, along with the §115 itemized statement. The statute's delivery rule for the itemized statement still applies.

FAQ

Can a landlord evict a tenant who stays after the lease ends in Oklahoma?

If the tenant remains in possession without the landlord's consent after the expiration of the term of the rental agreement or its termination under the Oklahoma Residential Landlord and Tenant Act, the landlord "may immediately bring an action for possession and damages" (41 O.S. §111(D)). That is a court action. See the eviction process.

Does a fixed-term lease need a notice to end in Oklahoma?

Unless it was earlier terminated under the Act or the parties otherwise agreed, a tenancy for a definite term "expires on the ending date thereof without notice" (41 O.S. §111(C)).

How much can a landlord recover from a holdover tenant in Oklahoma?

The landlord may bring an action for possession and damages. If the holdover is willful and not in good faith, the landlord may also recover "an amount not more than twice the average monthly rental, computed and prorated on a daily basis, for each month or portion thereof that the tenant remains in possession" (41 O.S. §111(D)).

What happens if the landlord lets the tenant stay after the lease ends?

If the landlord consents to the tenant's continued occupancy, "a month-to-month tenancy is thus created, unless the parties otherwise agree" (41 O.S. §111(D)). The subsection doesn't say what counts as consent, so ask counsel.

Can a landlord change the locks on a holdover tenant in Oklahoma?

41 O.S. §123 says a tenant whose landlord wrongfully removes or excludes the tenant from possession may recover possession or terminate the rental agreement, and in either case recover not more than twice the average monthly rental or twice actual damages, whichever is greater. Use the court process instead.
