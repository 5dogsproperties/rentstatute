Can an Oklahoma Landlord Enter Without Permission? Entry Notice Rules (41 O.S. §128)

41 O.S. §128 last re-read on OSCN 2026-10-26; statute pack weekly check 2026-11-02 found no change · informational, not legal advice

Informational, not legal advice. This page summarizes what the statute says in plain English. It doesn't tell you how a court would rule on your facts. Talk to a licensed Oklahoma attorney before you act.

Quick answer

Short answer: In an emergency, an Oklahoma landlord may enter without the tenant's consent. Otherwise, give at least 1 day's notice and enter only at reasonable times, unless notice is impracticable (41 O.S. §128).

The tenant can't unreasonably refuse entry for inspections, repairs, services, or showings. The landlord can't abuse the right of access or use it to harass the tenant. If a tenant refuses lawful access, §128 lets the landlord seek an injunction or terminate the lease. Changing the locks is not one of the options.

What the law says

  • Tenant consent: the tenant can't unreasonably refuse entry so the landlord can inspect, make repairs, supply services, or show the unit. 41 O.S. §128 · last checked 2026-10-26
  • Emergency: the landlord may enter without the tenant's consent. 41 O.S. §128 · last checked 2026-10-26
  • Everything else: give at least 1 day's notice of the intent to enter, and enter only at reasonable times. The notice requirement doesn't apply where notice is impracticable. 41 O.S. §128 · last checked 2026-10-26
  • No abuse or harassment: the landlord may not abuse the right of access or use it to harass the tenant. 41 O.S. §128 · last checked 2026-10-26
  • Refused lawful access: the landlord may seek an injunction to compel access or terminate the lease. 41 O.S. §128 · last checked 2026-10-26

No self-help: a landlord who wrongfully removes or excludes a tenant (a lockout) faces the remedies in 41 O.S. §123: the tenant may sue to recover possession or end the lease, and in either case recover up to the greater of 2× the average monthly rent or 2× actual damages. 41 O.S. §123 · last checked 2026-10-26

What this page doesn't cover

  • The statute summary used for this page doesn't set a delivery method for entry notice or define "reasonable times." Put both in your lease and keep proof of each notice. If it matters in a dispute, ask counsel.
  • Tenant remedies for improper entry, and entry after a tenant abandons or surrenders the unit, aren't covered here. See the OK Landlord Hub entry notice playbook and talk to counsel.

Practice (not law)

Practice (not law): Require written repair requests (portal or email) in the lease, and reply the same day with a timeline. A written request and reply also give you a clean record of why and when you entered for a repair. The statute baseline is the 1-day notice above (41 O.S. §128).

Practice notes come from John Ogorzalek, 5 Dogs Properties (owned 300+ units since 2014). They are habits, not substitutes for the statute.

Checklist before you enter

  1. Is it an emergency? If yes, you may enter without consent. Write down what the emergency was.
  2. If not, give notice at least 1 day ahead with the date, a reasonable time window, the purpose (inspect, repair, supply services, or show), and who will enter.
  3. Keep a copy of the notice and a record of how you delivered it.
  4. Don't abuse the right of access or use it to harass the tenant (§128).
  5. If the tenant refuses lawful access, offer another time in writing, then talk to counsel about an injunction or termination under §128. Never change the locks.

FAQ

Can a landlord enter without permission in Oklahoma?

In an emergency, yes. Otherwise the landlord gives at least 1 day's notice and enters at reasonable times, unless notice is impracticable (41 O.S. §128). The tenant can't unreasonably refuse entry for inspections, repairs, services, or showings.

How much notice does an Oklahoma landlord have to give before entering?

At least 1 day, unless it's an emergency or notice is impracticable (41 O.S. §128).

Can I show the unit to prospective tenants before the lease ends?

Showing the unit is one of the purposes the tenant can't unreasonably refuse under 41 O.S. §128. Give at least 1 day's notice and come at reasonable times.

What can I do if my tenant won't let me in?

If the tenant refuses lawful access, 41 O.S. §128 lets the landlord seek an injunction to compel access or terminate the lease. Talk to counsel first. Don't change the locks: a lockout triggers the tenant remedies in 41 O.S. §123.
