Can an Oklahoma Landlord Retaliate After a Repair Complaint?

Residential Landlord and Tenant Act (41 O.S.) on OSCN and bill text on oklegislature.gov · last checked 2026-10-19 · informational, not legal advice

Informational, not legal advice. This page describes what the statutes say and don't say. It doesn't tell you how a court would rule on your facts. Talk to a licensed Oklahoma attorney before you act.

Quick answer

Short answer: No general anti-retaliation section was found in Oklahoma's Residential Landlord and Tenant Act. MHA Oklahoma reported (Oct 27, 2026) that Oklahoma is one of only six states without such a law. Bills that would have added one failed. Fair housing and other laws may still apply in narrow cases.

That doesn't make timing irrelevant. A notice, non-renewal, or rent change right after a repair complaint can draw scrutiny from a tenant, a judge, or a fair-housing investigator. Prefer a short practical list? See the OK Landlord Hub checklist after a repair complaint.

What Title 41 says, and doesn't, about retaliation

We found no general anti-retaliation section in the RLTA (41 O.S. §101 et seq.). The RLTA has no section that bars a landlord from raising rent, cutting services, declining to renew, or filing for possession because a tenant complained about repairs.

One sign of that: HB2015 as introduced in the 2025-26 session proposed a new section, 41 O.S. §137, "to be codified." Lawmakers would not need a new section if one already existed. HB2015 as introduced (PDF) · last checked 2026-10-19

This is a description of what we found, not a ruling. Courts and other laws can still matter, as the next section explains.

Laws that may still apply

  • Federal Fair Housing Act: 42 U.S.C. §3617 makes it unlawful to coerce, intimidate, threaten, or interfere with anyone exercising fair-housing rights. That covers retaliation for a fair-housing complaint. It is not a general shield for repair complaints. See the OK Landlord Hub page on fair housing and families with kids.
  • Domestic violence lease termination: 41 O.S. §111(F) gives a tenant the right to end the lease without penalty under listed conditions (written notice plus a protective order within 30 days of the incident, unless the landlord waives). It is the tenant's lease-end right. It is not a retaliation rule. 41 O.S. §111 · last checked 2026-10-19
  • Subsidized, LIHTC, or CARES Act-covered housing: program rules, regulatory agreements, or federal notice rules may add requirements. We have not verified them for this page; ask counsel.
  • Lockouts: 41 O.S. §123 covers wrongful removal or exclusion. The tenant may recover possession or end the lease, and recover up to the greater of 2× average monthly rent or 2× actual damages. That is a lockout remedy, not a retaliation rule. 41 O.S. §123 · last checked 2026-10-19

Bills that tried to add a retaliation rule

HB2109 (2023)

KTUL reported that HB2109 would have added anti-retaliation protections and failed to advance. The same report quotes Katie Dilks of the Oklahoma Access to Justice Foundation saying Oklahoma's 1978 act left out the model act's anti-retaliation protections. (reported by KTUL, March 11, 2024)

HB2015 (2025-26 session)

  • As introduced: proposed a new 41 O.S. §137 for landlords renting more than ten dwelling units. It would have barred retaliation (rent increases, decreased services, possession actions, termination) after good-faith complaints, repair notices, tenant organizing, or testimony, with listed exceptions and a retaliation defense to possession. HB2015 introduced text (PDF) · last checked 2026-10-19
  • As passed by the House: the engrossed bill (52-39 on 03/26/2026) dropped §137 entirely and only amended 41 O.S. §121, tenant remedies for landlord breach. The bill that passed the House was a tenant-remedies bill, not an anti-retaliation bill. HB2015 engrossed text (PDF) · last checked 2026-10-19
  • Status: dead. Engrossed to the Senate 03/30/2026 and referred to Senate Judiciary 04/01/2026, where it died without a hearing. 2026 was the second and final session of the 60th Legislature, so it cannot carry over. HB2015 bill history · details on our 2026 Oklahoma law changes page.

How MHA and advocates frame it

  • MHA Oklahoma, urging lawmakers to protect tenants from retaliation: "Oklahoma is one of only six states without a state law protecting tenants from landlord retaliation." (reported by MHA Oklahoma, Oct 27, 2026) MHA did not list the six states; the figure is theirs, not ours.
  • OK Policy Institute made the same claim in a fact sheet, saying 44 states and the District of Columbia protect tenants from retaliation. (OK Policy fact sheet, Sept 2022)
  • KTUL's report on HB2109 quotes Oklahoma Access to Justice Foundation's Katie Dilks. (reported by KTUL, March 11, 2024)

These are advocacy and news sources, cited for what they reported.

Repair complaint, then notice or eviction: how they intersect

A repair complaint doesn't change the notice statutes. They work the same way before and after a complaint:

  • Unpaid rent: 5 days after written notice of the rent demand. 41 O.S. §131 · last checked 2026-10-19
  • Other remediable breach: 10 days after written notice to remedy. 41 O.S. §132 · last checked 2026-10-19
  • Month-to-month: 30 days' written notice. 41 O.S. §111 · last checked 2026-10-19

Full steps are on the Oklahoma eviction process page and the Hub before-you-file checklist. Whether a tenant can raise retaliation as a defense in an Oklahoma forcible entry and detainer case is not answered by the statutes we checked, and we pulled no case law. Ask a licensed Oklahoma attorney.

Safe practice for small landlords

Practice, not law. These habits keep decisions fair and documented.

  • Log every repair request and your response, with dates.
  • Apply rent and renewal decisions consistently across tenants, and put them in writing.
  • Write down the reason and timing for each notice, rent change, or non-renewal.
  • Don't cut services or utilities, and don't lock anyone out.
  • Talk to counsel before you non-renew or file soon after a complaint.

FAQ

Does Oklahoma have a landlord retaliation law?

No general anti-retaliation section was found in Oklahoma's Residential Landlord and Tenant Act (Title 41). MHA Oklahoma reported (Oct 27, 2026) that Oklahoma is one of only six states without a state law protecting tenants from landlord retaliation; that figure is MHA's, and it did not name the other states. Other laws may still apply in narrow cases. Informational, not legal advice.

Can a landlord evict after a repair complaint in Oklahoma?

No general RLTA anti-retaliation bar was found. The usual notice rules still apply: 5 days after a written rent demand (41 O.S. §131), 10 days to remedy a remediable breach (§132), and 30 days to end a month-to-month tenancy (§111). Whether a tenant can raise retaliation as a defense in an Oklahoma eviction case: ask a licensed Oklahoma attorney.

Can a landlord raise rent after a complaint?

No general RLTA bar on that timing was found. Your lease terms and any federal or program rules still apply, and a rent change right after a complaint can look retaliatory even where no statute bars it. Ask counsel about your situation.

Did Oklahoma try to pass a retaliation law?

Yes. KTUL reported (March 11, 2024) that HB2109 (2023) would have added anti-retaliation protections but failed to advance. HB2015 (2025-26), as introduced, proposed a new §137 for landlords renting more than ten units, but the House-passed engrossed version (52-39, 03/26/2026) dropped that section and only amended tenant remedies under §121; it then died in Senate Judiciary. See the 2026 law changes page.

Does fair housing law protect complaints?

The federal Fair Housing Act (42 U.S.C. §3617) bars coercion, intimidation, threats, or interference with people exercising fair-housing rights, which covers retaliation for fair-housing complaints. It is not a general shield for repair complaints. See the Hub fair housing page.

Does Oklahoma's domestic violence lease statute bar retaliation?

No. 41 O.S. §111(F) gives a tenant the right to end the lease without penalty under listed conditions (written notice plus a protective order within 30 days of the incident, unless the landlord waives). It is the tenant's lease-end right, not a general retaliation rule.
