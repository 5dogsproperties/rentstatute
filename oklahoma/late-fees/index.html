Quick answer

Short answer: No Oklahoma statute sets a dollar amount or percentage for residential rent late fees. 41 O.S. §131, the late-rent section, doesn't mention late fees at all. A late fee is a lease term, and Oklahoma's contract rules decide whether it is enforceable: a contract penalty is void (15 O.S. §213), while an amount fixed as presumed damages is valid only when actual damages would be "impracticable or extremely difficult to fix" (15 O.S. §215(A)).

In Sun Ridge Investors, Ltd. v. Parker, 1998 OK 22, the Oklahoma Supreme Court held that a $5.00-per-day late charge, on top of a $20.00 late fee, was a penalty "in the absence of any evidence to the contrary showing actual costs incurred by the Landlord." The Court did not rule on the $20.00 flat fee. No case or statute reviewed here sets a "safe" late-fee number.

What the law says

  • 41 O.S. §131 is silent on late fees. It lets the landlord sue for unpaid rent "at any time" after it is due (§131(A)) and end the lease if the tenant doesn't pay "within five (5) days after written notice of landlord's demand for payment" (§131(B)). It says nothing about late fees, grace periods or late-fee amounts. 41 O.S. §131 · last checked 2026-11-17
  • Penalties are void (15 O.S. §213): "Except as expressly provided in Section 215 of this title, penalties imposed by contract for any non-performance thereof, are void." 15 O.S. §213 · last checked 2026-11-17
  • Liquidated damages (15 O.S. §215(A)): a contract term "providing for the payment of an amount which shall be presumed to be the amount of damage sustained by a breach" is valid "when, from the nature of the case, it would be impracticable or extremely difficult to fix the actual damage." §215(A) excludes contracts to purchase and sell real property. 15 O.S. §215 · last checked 2026-11-17
  • §215(B)'s 5% is not a rent rule. The 5% figure in §215(B) applies to "a real estate sales contract" (a percentage of the purchase price). It isn't a cap on residential rent late fees. 15 O.S. §215 · last checked 2026-11-17

Sun Ridge Investors v. Parker (1998 OK 22)

  • Facts (¶2): rent was $465 a month, due on the 1st with no grace period. If unpaid by the 3rd, the lease charged a $20.00 late fee plus $5.00 per day until paid. "There is no dispute about the $20.00 late fee; the issue is whether the $5.00 per day charge is permissible." Sun Ridge Investors, Ltd. v. Parker, 1998 OK 22, 956 P.2d 876 · last checked 2026-11-17
  • The test (¶8): the Court listed three criteria courts generally use to tell valid liquidated damages from a penalty: "1) the injury caused by the breach must be difficult or impossible to estimate accurately; 2) the parties must intend to provide for damages rather than for a penalty; 3) the sum stipulated must be a reasonable pre-breach estimate of the probable loss." Sun Ridge Investors, Ltd. v. Parker, 1998 OK 22, 956 P.2d 876 · last checked 2026-11-17
  • Evidence (¶12): whether actual damages can be ascertained "with a reasonable degree of accuracy" is "a question of law for the Court to determine from the evidence." The landlord "offered no evidence of their actual costs of collection, nor any evidence that such costs were in excess of the $20.00 late fee, which the tenants willingly paid." Sun Ridge Investors, Ltd. v. Parker, 1998 OK 22, 956 P.2d 876 · last checked 2026-11-17
  • Holding (¶13): "the $5.00 per day 'additional rent' sought to be imposed for late-payment or non-payment of rent is a penalty, in the absence of any evidence to the contrary showing actual costs incurred by the Landlord." The landlord's damages from late rent "are not so difficult to ascertain so as to fall within the exception to the statute." Calling the charge "additional rent" did not change the result (¶10, ¶13). Sun Ridge Investors, Ltd. v. Parker, 1998 OK 22, 956 P.2d 876 · last checked 2026-11-17
  • What the Court reversed, and what stood (¶14 and disposition): it reversed the trial court's $330 money judgment (made up of the per-day charges) and the attorney-fee award. The disposition reads "trial court's judgment reversed in part"; the opinion does not say it reversed the judgment for possession. The Court did not decide whether the $20.00 flat fee was valid. Sun Ridge Investors, Ltd. v. Parker, 1998 OK 22, 956 P.2d 876 · last checked 2026-11-17
  • Earlier case (¶1, ¶6): the Court took the case to resolve a conflict with North American Inv. Co. v. Lawson, 1993 OK CIV APP 73, 854 P.2d 384, where a $2.00-per-day late fee on $150.00 monthly rent was held an unconscionable and unenforceable penalty. Sun Ridge Investors, Ltd. v. Parker, 1998 OK 22, 956 P.2d 876 · last checked 2026-11-17

What this means for a lease (analysis, not a holding)

  • Sun Ridge is about a per-day charge with no evidence of the landlord's actual costs. It does not approve any particular flat fee, and it does not set a cap.
  • Because the Court looked for "evidence ... showing actual costs incurred by the Landlord" (¶13), records of what late payment actually costs you (staff time, collection costs) matter if a fee is ever challenged.
  • §131(B)'s five-day demand is a demand "for payment" of rent, and Sun Ridge treated a charge labeled "additional rent" as a penalty. Talk to counsel before putting late fees in a §131 demand. See the 5-day notice page. 41 O.S. §131 · last checked 2026-11-17

How experienced landlords do it

Practice notes come from John Ogorzalek, 5 Dogs Properties (owned 300+ units since 2014). They are habits, not substitutes for the statute.

Practice (not law): Wait until rent is 5 days late before serving the §131 demand, and put the due date and grace period in the lease. (§131 itself allows the demand once rent is unpaid when due.)

Bills in 2025 and 2026

We found no 2025 or 2026 Oklahoma bill on residential late fees that became law. The Oklahoma Legislature's Landlord and Tenant subject index for the 2025–2026 sessions lists no late-fee bill, and none of the listed bills was enacted. An earlier bill, HB 3095 (2024), included statutory late-fee limits as introduced, but the House committee substitute of 02/28/2024 removed them. The version that passed the House dealt with landlord retaliation; it was referred to Senate Judiciary on 03/27/2024 and went no further. The 2027 session convenes in February 2027.

What this page doesn't cover

  • Late fees in federally subsidized or voucher housing, or other program rules.
  • Usury or interest-rate arguments (raised by the tenants in Sun Ridge, ¶2, but not the basis of the holding).
  • Commercial leases and real estate sales contracts.

FAQ

Is there a legal limit on late fees in Oklahoma?

No Oklahoma statute sets a number. 41 O.S. §131 is silent on late fees. Courts apply the penalty and liquidated-damages rules in 15 O.S. §213 and 15 O.S. §215(A). In Sun Ridge Investors, Ltd. v. Parker, 1998 OK 22, 956 P.2d 876, a $5.00-per-day charge on top of a $20.00 late fee was held a penalty absent evidence of the landlord's actual costs.

Are daily late fees legal in Oklahoma?

In Sun Ridge Investors, Ltd. v. Parker, 1998 OK 22, 956 P.2d 876, the Oklahoma Supreme Court held a $5.00-per-day late charge on $465 rent, added to a $20.00 late fee, was a penalty in the absence of evidence showing the landlord's actual costs. Talk to counsel before using a per-day charge.

Is a $20 or 5% late fee automatically OK in Oklahoma?

No statute or case reviewed here says so. Sun Ridge did not rule on the $20.00 flat fee ("There is no dispute about the $20.00 late fee"), and the 5% figure in 15 O.S. §215(B) applies only to real estate sales contracts.

Does Oklahoma require a grace period for rent?

41 O.S. §131 does not set one. It allows a written demand once rent is unpaid when due and lets the landlord end the lease if the tenant doesn't pay within 5 days after the demand.
