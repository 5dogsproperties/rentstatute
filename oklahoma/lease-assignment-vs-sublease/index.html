Lease Assignment vs Sublease: What Oklahoma Landlords Need to Know

41 O.S. §10 and §11 re-read on OSCN 2026-11-06 against Research Bot review · informational, not legal advice

Informational, not legal advice. This page summarizes what the statutes and cited cases say in plain English. It doesn't tell you how a court would rule on your facts. Talk to a licensed Oklahoma attorney before you act — especially for leases longer than two years or commercial units.

Quick answer

Short answer: Under 41 O.S. §10, a tenant with a term of two years or less, at will, or by sufferance may not assign or transfer the lease interest or any part thereof without the landlord's written assent. Section 10 does not use the word “sublease.” Common-law definitions in Snow v. Winn, 1980 OK 27, 607 P.2d 678, distinguish an assignment (remainder of the term) from a sublease (less than the whole term). The original tenant generally stays liable unless there is an agreement to release (Buck v. J.M. McEntee & Sons, 1954 OK 286, 275 P.2d 984 — commercial restaurant lease facts; Oklahoma Supreme Court authority on assignment liability, not an RLTA holding).

Assignment vs sublease — the difference

Oklahoma's §10 never defines “assignment” or “sublease,” and it never says the word “sublease.” The labels landlords use come from case law. In Snow v. Winn, 1980 OK 27, 607 P.2d 678, the Oklahoma Supreme Court explained:

  • Assignment: a transfer of the whole or some specific part of the leasehold estate for the entire remainder of the lease term.
  • Sublease: a transfer of the whole or a portion of the leasehold estate for a period that extends for less than the whole term.

The label on the paper does not control; the substance does. A document titled “sublease” that hands over the rest of the term may still be treated as an assignment, and the reverse can happen too. Ask counsel if the paperwork is ambiguous.

What 41 O.S. §10 actually says

OSCN text (CiteID 71705, last checked 2026-11-06):

“No tenant for a term not exceeding two (2) years, or at will, or by sufferance, shall assign or transfer his term or interest, or any part thereof, to another, without the written assent of the landlord or person holding under him.”

  • Written assent is the statute word — not a paraphrase into a different rule. Verbal “ok” is not what §10 describes.
  • Who it covers: tenants with a term of two years or less, tenants at will, and tenants by sufferance.
  • “Or any part thereof”: the statute reaches a transfer of part of the interest, not only a full handoff of the whole remaining term. That is how partial transfers and shorter handoffs are discussed under §10 — not because the statute invents a separate “subletting” rule. Section 10 never says “sublease.”
  • Leases longer than two years: §10's written-assent rule as quoted above is scoped to the tenancies it lists. Your lease clause still controls; ask counsel for longer terms and for commercial units.

If a tenant assigns or transfers without written assent

After a §10 violation, 41 O.S. §11 says the landlord, after giving ten (10) days' notice to quit possession, shall have a right to reenter the premises and take possession, and dispossess the tenant, subtenant, or undertenant. 41 O.S. §11 · CiteID 71706 · last checked 2026-11-06

No self-help lockout. “Right to reenter” is not permission to change the locks or remove people without the court. Use the forcible entry and detainer process. See the Oklahoma eviction process page. Wrongful removal or exclusion can trigger tenant remedies under 41 O.S. §123.

Do not conflate §11's ten-day notice-to-quit clock with the remediable-breach clocks in 41 O.S. §132. An unauthorized occupant or lease breach may also implicate a §132 lease-violation path. Which notice fits your facts is a counsel question — this page does not pick the path for you. Related: lease violation notice (§132).

Is the original tenant still on the hook?

There is no automatic release when a lease is assigned. In Buck v. J.M. McEntee & Sons, 1954 OK 286, 275 P.2d 984, the Oklahoma Supreme Court held (syllabus ¶3) that a lessee is not relieved of liability by an assignment even with the lessor's consent, unless there is an agreement to accept the assignee and release the lessee.

Commercial-facts note: Buck involved a commercial restaurant lease (a three-year term), not an RLTA dwelling-unit dispute. Treat it as Oklahoma Supreme Court authority on assignment liability — not as an RLTA holding. Prefer a written release as practice; Buck requires an agreement to release, not a magic form word alone.

  • Full release (practice): landlord agrees in writing to accept the assignee and release the original tenant from future rent/performance (past amounts may still be owed).
  • Partial release (practice): release only for future rent, or only after the assignee cures listed conditions.
  • No release (practice): original tenant stays jointly responsible; keep that clear in the assent document.

When a new lease is cleaner

Practice, not law.

Many small owners skip assignment paperwork and sign a new lease with the incoming person when:

  • The current tenant is leaving for good and wants a full release.
  • Rent, term, pets, occupants, or other material terms are changing.
  • Little time is left on the old lease and a fresh start is simpler.
  • You want a new deposit and a clean move-in condition record.

A new lease is an owner choice, not a statutory requirement. Document the end of the old tenancy and the start of the new one in writing.

Handling the deposit in a handoff

41 O.S. §115 escrow and return rules still apply to any deposit you hold. See the Oklahoma security deposit page for §115(A)–(B) detail — this page does not duplicate that statute.

Do not cite §115(C) for tenant assignment or sublease handoffs. Section 115(C) addresses cessation of the landlord's interest (sale, landlord-side assignment, death, and similar), not a tenant handing the unit to someone else.

Practice (not law): Decide the deposit plan in writing before anyone moves. Common options: (1) refund the original tenant under §115 and take a new deposit from the incoming person, or (2) transfer the existing deposit with a signed acknowledgment from original tenant, incoming person, and landlord stating the amount and who is responsible for claims.

Screening the incoming person

Use the same written screening criteria you use for any applicant, and apply them consistently. Fair housing rules still apply; see the Hub page on fair housing and families with kids. This page does not state a settled Oklahoma residential rule that a silent lease implies (or does not imply) a reasonableness limit on withholding assent — that claim is secondary-source only. Prefer: lease terms control; use consistent criteria; ask counsel if you are asked about reasonableness.

Landlord checklist (short)

Practice habits. The Hub companion expands each step.

  1. Get a written request naming the current tenant, unit, proposed person, and dates.
  2. Screen the incoming person with your usual written criteria (fair housing consistent).
  3. Put written assent in a dated document naming person, unit, and dates.
  4. Use an assignment-and-assumption agreement, or a carefully documented shorter-term handoff (call it a sublease only with Snow definitions — not as a §10 word).
  5. Decide release: full, partial, or none — in writing.
  6. Decide the deposit plan in writing (§115(A)–(B); not §115(C)).
  7. Record move-in condition; update renters insurance / guarantor if your lease requires it (practice).

FAQ

Can a tenant sublet without asking in Oklahoma?

41 O.S. §10 does not use the word “sublease.” For a tenancy of two years or less, at will, or by sufferance, it requires the landlord's written assent before the tenant assigns or transfers the lease interest or any part thereof. Common-law labels for assignment vs sublease come from Snow v. Winn, 1980 OK 27, 607 P.2d 678. Informational, not legal advice.

Does assent have to be in writing?

Yes for tenancies covered by 41 O.S. §10. The statute requires “written assent” of the landlord (or person holding under the landlord) before the tenant assigns or transfers the term or interest, or any part thereof.

Can I refuse a lease assignment or handoff request?

Your lease terms control what you promised. Apply the same written screening criteria you use for any applicant and follow fair housing rules. Whether a silent lease implies a reasonableness limit is not treated here as settled Oklahoma residential law; ask a licensed Oklahoma attorney.

Is the original tenant released after an assignment in Oklahoma?

Not automatically. In Buck v. J.M. McEntee & Sons, 1954 OK 286, 275 P.2d 984 (a commercial restaurant lease), the Oklahoma Supreme Court held a lessee is not relieved by assignment even with lessor consent unless there is an agreement to accept the assignee and release the lessee. Prefer a written release as practice. Not an RLTA dwelling-unit holding.

What if the tenant already moved someone in without written assent?

After a §10 violation, 41 O.S. §11 lets the landlord give ten days' notice to quit possession and then seek to reenter and dispossess the tenant, subtenant, or undertenant. That still requires court process — do not lock the person out. See the Oklahoma eviction process page. Ask counsel which notice path fits your facts.

Who gets the security deposit back after a lease handoff?

Document the plan in writing. 41 O.S. §115(A)–(B) still govern any deposit you hold (escrow and the 45-day return rules). Do not treat §115(C) as a tenant-handoff rule; that subsection addresses cessation of the landlord's interest. Practice options include refunding the original tenant and taking a new deposit, or transferring with a signed acknowledgment.
