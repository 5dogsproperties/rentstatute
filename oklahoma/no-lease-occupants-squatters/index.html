Oklahoma Squatters and Occupants With No Lease: What 41 O.S. §111 and 21 O.S. §§1354–1357 Say

41 O.S. §111 last re-read on OSCN 2026-11-10 (CiteID 71758); 21 O.S. §§1354–1357 from enrolled SB 1994, last checked 2026-11-10 · informational, not legal advice

Informational, not legal advice. This page summarizes what the statute says in plain English. It doesn't tell you how a court would rule on your facts. Talk to a licensed Oklahoma attorney before you act.

Quick answer

Short answer: It depends on whether the person ever had your consent. Under 41 O.S. §111(G), if someone is in the unit with no rental agreement and no consent from the landlord, the landlord may demand that they leave, and no eviction case is needed. Refusing to leave within a reasonable time is trespass, punishable on conviction by a fine of up to $500.

If you agreed to let someone live there and take rent with no written lease, that is usually a tenancy, not squatting. 41 O.S. §102(12) defines a "rental agreement" as "all agreements," not just written ones, and §102(15) defines a tenant as a person entitled under a rental agreement to occupy a dwelling unit. 21 O.S. §1354(A)(5) also treats current or former tenants under a written or oral rental agreement as outside the sheriff-removal process. A month-to-month or at-will tenancy ends with 30 days' written notice (41 O.S. §111(A)), and if they stay, possession goes through the court eviction process. Applying §111(G) to former guests is risky. Don't lock out anyone who might be a tenant (41 O.S. §123). A separate 2024 statute (21 O.S. §§1354–1357) lets a property owner request sheriff removal if all nine §1354(A) conditions are met; after the sheriff's notice, the owner may change locks and remove the occupant's property. That process shall not be used to circumvent Title 41.

What the law says

  • No agreement and no consent (§111(G)): the landlord may demand that the occupant leave. No eviction case is needed. Refusing to leave within a reasonable time is trespass, punishable on conviction by a fine of up to $500. 41 O.S. §111 · last checked 2026-11-10
  • Month-to-month or at will (§111(A)): ending it takes 30 days' written notice. The 30 days run from the date of service. 41 O.S. §111 · last checked 2026-11-10
  • Shorter than month-to-month (§111(B)) (for example week-to-week): 7 days' written notice. 41 O.S. §111 · last checked 2026-11-10
  • Serving the notice (§111(E)), in this order: personal delivery to the tenant; if the tenant can't be found, a family member over 12 who lives there; otherwise post in a conspicuous place AND mail a copy by certified mail or USPS Firm Mailing Book for Accountable Mail. 41 O.S. §111 · last checked 2026-11-10
  • If a tenant stays after proper notice: possession goes through a forcible entry and detainer (FED) case in district court, after the §111 notice. See the eviction process. 12 O.S. §1148.1 et seq. · last checked 2026-10-19

No self-help: if the person turns out to be a tenant, a landlord who wrongfully removes or excludes them (a lockout) faces the remedies in 41 O.S. §123: the tenant may sue to recover possession or end the lease, and in either case recover up to the greater of 2× the average monthly rent or 2× actual damages. Don't shut off utilities either. After a proper sheriff notice under 21 O.S. §1354, the owner may change the locks and remove the unlawful occupant's personal property (§1354(D)); a wrongful removal can bring restored possession, actual damages, statutory damages equal to triple the fair market rent, court costs, and reasonable attorney fees (§1354(E)). 41 O.S. §123; 21 O.S. §1354(D)–(E) · last checked 2026-11-10

Which situation are you in?

  • Never had your consent (moved in without permission, no agreement): §111(G) is the starting point. Make the demand to leave in writing and keep a copy.
  • You agreed, but nothing is in writing (paid rent, month-to-month): treat it as a tenancy. Serve the 30-day written notice under §111(A) and §111(E). See the 30-day notice.
  • A guest, partner, or relative of your tenant who stayed on: the statute pack flags applying §111(G) to "guests" as risky. Talk to counsel before treating them as having no consent.
  • A tenant whose lease ended: that's a holdover, not a squatter. Under §111(D), the landlord may sue for possession right away, and if you consent to them staying, the tenancy becomes month-to-month. See the eviction timeline. 41 O.S. §111 · last checked 2026-11-10

Sheriff removal of unlawful occupants (21 O.S. §§1354–1357)

Oklahoma enacted a separate squatter and unlawful-occupant sheriff-removal statute in 2024: SB 1994 (Laws 2024, c. 378), approved June 5, 2024, with an emergency clause, effective on approval, and codified at 21 O.S. §§1354–1357. This is not a how-to for every fact pattern. Talk to counsel before you use it.

  • All nine conditions in 21 O.S. §1354(A) must be met before a property owner or authorized agent may request the county sheriff to immediately remove persons unlawfully occupying real property:
    1. The requesting person is the property owner or authorized agent of the property owner.
    2. Unauthorized persons have unlawfully entered and remain or continue to reside on the property.
    3. The real property was not open to members of the public at the time they entered.
    4. The property owner or agent has already directed them to leave.
    5. They are not current or former tenants pursuant to a written or oral rental agreement authorized by the owner.
    6. They cannot produce government address-of-record documents (including, but not limited to, Service Oklahoma or the Election Board) showing they used the property address as an address of record within the previous 12 months.
    7. They cannot produce a lease signed by the property owner or agent.
    8. They are not immediate family members of the property owner.
    9. There is no pending litigation related to the real property between the property owner and any known unauthorized person.
    21 O.S. §1354 · last checked 2026-11-10
  • Verified complaint (§1354(B)): the request is a completed, verified complaint form. 21 O.S. §1354 · last checked 2026-11-10
  • Sheriff's notice (§1354(C)): if the sheriff verifies the requester is the record owner or authorized agent and otherwise entitled to relief, the sheriff must, without unnecessary delay, serve a notice to immediately vacate and put the owner in possession. 21 O.S. §1354 · last checked 2026-11-10
  • After that notice (§1354(D)): the owner may ask the sheriff to stand by while the owner changes the locks and removes the unlawful occupants' personal property. The owner is not liable for loss of that property unless the removal was wrongful. 21 O.S. §1354 · last checked 2026-11-10
  • Wrongful removal (§1354(E)): the removed person may sue to be restored to possession and recover actual costs and damages, statutory damages equal to triple the fair market rent, court costs, and reasonable attorney fees. 21 O.S. §1354 · last checked 2026-11-10
  • May not circumvent Title 41 (21 O.S. §1357): this process shall not be used to circumvent Title 41 landlord-tenant rights. If there was a written or oral tenancy, use §111 notice and a forcible entry and detainer case, not the sheriff complaint. 21 O.S. §1357 · last checked 2026-11-10

What this page doesn't cover

  • Oklahoma criminal trespass law generally, and when or whether police will remove someone outside the 21 O.S. §§1354–1357 sheriff process described above. Call local law enforcement and counsel.
  • "Squatters' rights" in the sense of adverse possession (claiming title by long occupation). That is outside the Landlord-Tenant Act and isn't covered here.
  • What counts as a "reasonable time" to leave after a §111(G) demand. The statute summary used here doesn't define it.
  • Short-term and vacation-rental guests. The Act excludes transient occupancy in a hotel, motel, or similar lodging regardless of the length of the stay (41 O.S. §104(4), Laws 2023, c. 159), and it doesn't name Airbnb/VRBO-type rentals. Ask counsel.

Practice (not law)

Practice (not law): Hand notices only to an adult 18 or older. If no adult is present, post AND mail. This is stricter than the statute, which allows a household family member over 12 (41 O.S. §111(E)).

Checklist before you act

  1. Write down how the person got in, whether you (or a prior manager) ever agreed to it, and whether anyone paid or accepted rent.
  2. No agreement and no consent: give a written demand to leave under §111(G), keep a copy, and note the date and time. If all nine 21 O.S. §1354(A) conditions are met, a property owner or authorized agent may also request sheriff removal under that section; do not use it to circumvent Title 41 (§1357). Talk to counsel.
  3. Any agreement or accepted rent: serve a 30-day written notice (month-to-month) or 7-day notice (shorter periods) under §111, served as §111(E) lists.
  4. Not sure which applies, or the person was a guest: talk to counsel before treating them as a trespasser.
  5. Don't change the locks, remove belongings, or shut off utilities if the person might be a tenant (§123; §121(C)). After a proper sheriff notice under 21 O.S. §1354, the owner may change locks and remove the unlawful occupant's property (§1354(D)); a wrongful removal carries restored possession, actual damages, triple fair-market rent, costs, and fees (§1354(E)).
  6. If they stay after proper notice, file in court. See the OK Landlord Hub eviction pre-filing checklist.

FAQ

Do you need to evict a squatter in Oklahoma?

If the person is there with no rental agreement and no consent from the landlord, 41 O.S. §111(G) lets the landlord demand that they leave without an eviction case, and refusing to leave within a reasonable time is trespass, punishable on conviction by a fine of up to $500. If you ever agreed to let them stay or accepted rent, treat them as a tenant and use the notice and court process. Talk to counsel.

How do I evict someone with no lease in Oklahoma?

If they live there with your consent but without a written lease, the tenancy is usually month-to-month or at will. Serve 30 days' written notice under 41 O.S. §111(A) (7 days for shorter periods). If they stay after the notice period, file a forcible entry and detainer case.

Can I change the locks on a squatter in Oklahoma?

If the person turns out to be a tenant, a lockout is wrongful exclusion under 41 O.S. §123, with damages up to the greater of 2× average monthly rent or 2× actual damages. After a proper sheriff notice under 21 O.S. §1354, the owner may change the locks and remove the unlawful occupant's property (§1354(D)); a wrongful removal can bring restored possession, actual damages, triple fair-market rent, costs, and fees (§1354(E)). Self-help lockout of a tenant is still barred. Talk to counsel.

Is a guest who won't leave a squatter?

Not necessarily. The statute pack flags applying 41 O.S. §111(G) to guests as risky. Talk to counsel before treating a former guest as having no consent.
